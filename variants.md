Judge brief (in priority order): (1) really one line on a free/entertaining theme, (2) stops the
scroll in the first few words, (3) genuinely entertaining, not merely clever or trying hard,
(4) lands in a single sentence with a turn/punch at the end, (5) feels fresh.

Research applied (see builder-report.md): compression — every word load-bearing; plant info
early, reframe it at the end; avoid famous classics (anti-gravity book, soap, stairs, atoms)
because freshness is scored and the judge has met them a hundred times; concrete and specific
beats generic (proven lesson from our past results).

## Head-to-head (top 4)

- **Semi 1: #1 Plants vs #11 Headphones** — Plants wins. Both land in one sentence, but
  Plants has a real-world incongruity (went away to learn care, killed the things needing
  care) while Headphones is a neat observation with a softer second half.
- **Semi 2: #8 Dentist vs #9 Password manager** — Password manager wins. The dentist's
  "four thousand times" is vivid, but the lockout loop is tighter: the tool built to
  remember for you defeats you, and the punch ("stronger than my memory") reframes the
  whole sentence.
- **Final: #1 Plants vs #9 Password manager** — **Plants wins.** The password-manager line
  is a clean paradox, but it sits in the familiar tech-frustration shape the judge will
  have seen many times. Plants is the more surprising scene: a wellness retreat — the
  place you go to get better at caring for living things — is exactly what killed them.
  The twist ("living things" = the plants) arrives only at the last two words, the opening
  ("My plants died") stops the scroll, and nothing in it is a known joke.
